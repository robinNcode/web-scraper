## Abstract  
The article examines a logical tension in classical Islamic theology: Allah is defined as a wholly self‑sufficient, unconditioned being, while the Qur’an is simultaneously presented as an uncreated attribute of Him and as a text whose specific verses respond to particular human persons, questions, and historical events. By analyzing Qur’anic verses, Sunni creed statements, and hadith that portray the Qur’an as a conscious advocate pleading before Allah, the author argues that the Qur’an’s contingent content renders a supposedly unconditioned divine attribute dependent on creation, thereby challenging the coherence of Allah’s claimed non‑contingency.

## 1. Introduction  
Classical monotheistic philosophy, including Islamic scholasticism, characterises God as “parm, amukhāpekṣita” (supreme and non‑contingent). In Sunni orthodoxy the Qur’an is affirmed as Allah’s “kalam” and an “anadi” (uncreated, eternal) attribute. The problem addressed is whether this claim can coexist with the observable fact that Qur’anic verses are tied to concrete human realities—specific individuals, legal cases, wars, and social disputes. The purpose is to reveal a possible inconsistency between Allah’s aseity and the Qur’an’s human‑dependent content.

## 2. Key Content  

### 2.1 Classical Definition of Divine Non‑Contingency  
- God is described as **‘wājib al‑wujūd’** – existence requiring no external cause.  
- The term **‘ginaa’** is used in Islamic theology to denote this unconditioned nature.  

### 2.2 Qur’an as an Uncreated Attribute  
- Sunni creed declares the Qur’an to be Allah’s speech, an eternal, non‑created sifat.  

### 2.3 Human‑Dependent Substance of the Qur’an  
- The Qur’an contains guidance, law, answers to questions, and rulings on marriage, inheritance, war, etc.  
- Numerous verses are linked to particular persons (e.g., Abu Lahab) or events; removing those persons/events would alter the verse’s specific meaning.  

### 2.4 Logical Consequence  
- If a Qur’anic verse originates from a human circumstance, its content is **contingent** on that circumstance.  
- Consequently, an attribute that is declared unconditioned becomes, in practice, dependent on created reality.  

### 2.5 Hadith Illustration of Qur’an’s Agency  
- Hadith describe the Qur’an **appearing on the Day of Judgment**, recommending, pleading, and addressing Allah on behalf of individuals.  
- Example: Qur’an says, “O Lord, adorn him…,” then seeks Allah’s satisfaction, showing a **conscious, purposeful role**.  

### 2.6 The Central Thesis  
- The coexistence of Allah’s absolute non‑contingency with a Qur’an whose specific content is human‑contingent is logically incoherent.
